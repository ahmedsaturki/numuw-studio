# NUMUW Competitive & Best-Practice Benchmark

## Purpose

NUMUW should learn from proven patterns instead of inventing unnecessary mechanics. The goal is not to copy another company's visual language; it is to borrow validated principles and adapt them to an Egyptian, founder-led growth studio.

## Reference models

### Demand Curve — problem-led positioning

Demand Curve currently frames its audience around recognizable growth problems, including teams that are growing without a repeatable system, lack senior growth leadership, or have been burned by agencies that spent budget without moving the needle.

**Lesson for NUMUW:** lead with the business situation and desired decision, not a catalog of services. The homepage therefore routes from problem → diagnosis → product rather than presenting a generic service wall.

Source: https://www.demandcurve.com/

### Reforge — expertise as an asset

Reforge positions its value around vetted operators, real examples, guides and practical playbooks rather than a collection of disconnected tactics.

**Lesson for NUMUW:** the Business Library, playbooks, diagnostic tools and evidence policy are part of the product experience, not decorative extras.

Source: https://www.reforge.com/

### B2B agency benchmarks — buying complexity

A 2026 review of leading B2B website agencies emphasizes that B2B websites must support multiple decision makers, not only visual polish: technical evaluators, financial decision makers and executive sponsors may all need different information.

**Lesson for NUMUW:** sector pages should explain the buying context, objections, scope and evidence, not merely change the headline and keywords.

Source: https://www.blendb2b.com/resources/best-website-agencies-for-b2b-growth

### Baymard — evidence over opinion

Baymard's large-scale ecommerce research repeatedly finds that flow design, clarity and usability materially affect checkout completion, and its 2026 research continues to update benchmarks using real-user testing and quantitative studies.

**Lesson for NUMUW:** when a page or tool influences a decision, reduce friction at the exact decision point. Avoid adding interfaces just because they look impressive.

Sources:
- https://baymard.com/research/checkout-usability
- https://baymard.com/research-articles/ecommerce-quantitative-ux-insights-2026

### Google Search Central — people first

Google's current guidance emphasizes helpful, reliable, people-first content and warns against producing large amounts of search-engine-first content across many topics without adding meaningful value.

**Lesson for NUMUW:** every landing page must have a real audience, problem, buying context, useful depth and a different reason to exist. Page count is not a quality metric.

Source: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content

### Google structured data

Google recommends relevant Organization information on the home/about experience, accurate markup that reflects page content, and validation before relying on structured data.

**Lesson for NUMUW:** use structured data conservatively, only for information that is real and visible/appropriate; validate it as a release gate.

Sources:
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization
- https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/breadcrumb

### W3C WCAG 2.2 — accessible by default

WCAG 2.2 includes Focus Not Obscured and Target Size requirements and provides techniques for strong/two-color focus indicators, reflow and scroll-padding.

**Lesson for NUMUW:** accessibility is part of component quality, not a last-minute audit. Shared controls must make keyboard focus visible and avoid sticky UI hiding focused content.

Source: https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/new-in-22/

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- service-list homepages that make the buyer decide what to buy before understanding the problem
- duplicate landing pages with keyword substitutions and no new decision value
- fake testimonials, logos, awards or outcome claims
- giant dashboards or calculators that create activity but not decisions
- excessive animations, third-party widgets and tracking that increase complexity without business value
- vendor lock-in where the client cannot own domains, accounts, source or operating documentation
- treating a click, lead or page visit as revenue proof
- shipping a repository-perfect build and calling live production verified without browser/network evidence

## NUMUW adaptation rule

For every proposed new feature, answer:

1. What real buyer or operator problem does it solve?
2. What evidence says this pattern works?
3. Which existing NUMUW component can solve it before adding a new one?
4. Does it improve a decision, a conversion, delivery confidence or operational reliability?
5. What new complexity, privacy, accessibility and performance cost does it add?
6. How will we verify it before release?
