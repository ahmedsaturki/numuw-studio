# NUMUW Competitive / Pattern Benchmark

## Principle

We do not copy competitors. We extract reusable patterns from proven businesses and standards, then adapt them to NUMUW's market, proof level and operating model.

## Pattern 1 — Outcome-led positioning

Regional B2B players increasingly position around the commercial outcome rather than a menu of services.

Observed examples:
- VadeCom frames its offer from first click to closed deal and emphasizes unified assets, CRM, tracking and B2B pipeline.
- Meriin frames growth around revenue efficiency, attribution, measurement and a senior-led system.
- Mahmoud Essawy frames the offer around measurable growth targets, a 90-day scorecard and executive ownership.

NUMUW implication:
- lead with the business constraint
- make the product the commercial unit
- keep capabilities underneath the product rather than presenting a flat service catalogue

## Pattern 2 — Diagnose before prescribing

CXL describes a repeatable conversion process using research and evidence rather than isolated tactics.
OneKind describes a sequence of Measure → Diagnose → Test → Scale.
NUMUW implication:
- Solution Finder = lightweight routing
- Diagnostic = deeper investigation
- Product = scoped intervention
- Growth Partner = ongoing learning loop

## Pattern 3 — Customer-journey thinking

McKinsey's B2B work emphasizes customer needs, important journeys, governance and cross-functional execution. It also shows why technology alone does not fix bad data or broken processes.

NUMUW implication:
- connect marketing, conversion and operations
- do not sell automation when the process itself is broken
- define ownership and acceptance, not just implementation

## Pattern 4 — Evidence over logo walls

Case-study-led firms use measurable evidence where they have it. NUMUW is not yet in a position to invent proof, so the correct strategy is to show:
- public tools
- public documents
- explicit process
- clear acceptance
- real cases only when evidence is available

## Pattern 5 — People-first content

Google's guidance explicitly asks whether a site has a primary purpose, serves an intended audience, demonstrates first-hand expertise, and gives a satisfying answer. Large amounts of near-duplicate or search-first pages are a warning sign.

NUMUW implication:
- every landing page must solve a real decision problem
- reduce page sprawl when it stops adding value
- build deeper useful content before adding more URLs

## Pattern 6 — Navigation must match mental models

NN/G research emphasizes that labels and categories need user testing and that site structure should help people understand where they are.

NUMUW implication:
- global navigation is stable
- human site map is a secondary orientation layer
- breadcrumbs help inner pages
- section-specific menus are not allowed to contradict global architecture

## Pattern 7 — Performance and accessibility are product quality

Current web guidance treats LCP, INP and CLS as field UX metrics. WCAG 2.2 requires visible focus and protects users from focused elements being obscured.

NUMUW implication:
- source budget is necessary but insufficient
- live browser/Lighthouse/field validation remains a separate gate
- no visual flourish should compromise performance or accessibility
